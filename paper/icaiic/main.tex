\begin{abstract}
Post-hoc explanations are attached to wearable stress classifiers, but
systematic reviews of XAI evaluation exclude run time from their scope,
studies that explain such classifiers report accuracy without explanation
time, and deployment studies of physiological monitoring measure transport
metrics without explanations. We deploy a frozen \NParams-parameter 1D-CNN trained
on WESAD as a TCP client--server system, run its server co-located with
the client on a laptop, on a Raspberry~Pi~4 fog node and on a cloud VPS,
and measure the seven metrics used in fog-computing studies with an
open-loop sensor client and drift-corrected clock synchronisation.
Without explanation the server pipeline takes a median of
\ColocNoneExecMed\ms per 10\,s window on the laptop and \FogNoneExecMed\ms
on the Pi; a single region-based LIME or SHAP (GradientExplainer)
explanation takes \ColocXaiMin--\ColocXaiMax\ms and
\FogXaiMin--\FogXaiMax\ms respectively,
\XaiShareMin--\XaiShareMax\% of the execution time. LAN latency stays
below \LatMaxAll\ms and the Internet path adds
\CloudNetMin--\CloudNetMax\ms each way, whereas the Pi is
\FogOverColocXaiLimeC--\FogOverColocXaiShap\x slower per explanation
than the laptop and every SHAP-cheap response on the Pi exceeds the 1\,s
real-time bound: under explanation, placement is a compute decision. On a
\SelMinutes-minute segment in which \SelShapCRate\% of windows had
confidence below 0.80, explaining only those windows reduced the mean
execution time \SelExecGainMin--\SelExecGainMax\x and raised the throughput
capacity of one worker from \FogODShapCCap to \SelShapCCap concurrent users.
Gating directs explanations to errors only where confidence is calibrated:
at the same threshold it explains \CaughtElevenEighty of
\ErrorsElevenEighty misclassified windows of one validation subject but
\CaughtFourteenEighty of \ErrorsFourteenEighty of another.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ======================================================================

Classifiers for stress detection from wearable physiological signals reach
high reported accuracies on public datasets such as
WESAD~\cite{schmidt2018,almadhor2025}, and post-hoc explanations such as
LIME~\cite{ribeiro2016} and SHAP~\cite{lundberg2017} are attached to
them~\cite{almadhor2025,elrashidy2025}. These explanations are evaluated
for quality, not for cost: a systematic review of XAI
evaluation explicitly excludes run time from its scope~\cite{nauta2023},
and recent work that explains stress classifiers trained on WESAD reports
neither inference nor explanation time~\cite{almadhor2025}. In a real-time
monitor, a new 10\,s window arrives every 5\,s, and
the time spent explaining one window is unavailable to the next window
and to other users.

Fog-computing studies of physiological monitoring measure precisely such
system-level quantities---latency, queuing delay, execution time, response
time, jitter, bandwidth and resource usage---but without
explanations~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025,tuli2020healthfog}. Conversely,
measurements of explanation cost are made offline on workstation or
server hardware~\cite{shats2025,warnecke2020,jethani2022}. This paper
measures both in one budget, on a resource-constrained fog node of the kind
used in those studies. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An end-to-end measurement of explanation cost inside a TCP
client--server stress detection pipeline at three placements (laptop, fog, cloud), using the seven
metrics of~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025}, an open-loop load generator and
drift-corrected clock synchronisation (Sec.~\ref{sec:system}).
\item Evidence that explanation accounts for \XaiShareMin--\XaiShareMax\%
of server execution time and that, once it is enabled, placement matters
through compute rather than network (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-cost},
\ref{sec:res-place}).
\item A measured characterisation of confidence-gated explanation on the
fog node---cost, throughput capacity and the queuing of several users---each
matched by a simple analytical model (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-selective},
\ref{sec:res-load}).
\item The calibration condition under which gating directs explanations to
errors, and the cost--stability trade-off between explainer operating
points (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-calib}, \ref{sec:res-quality}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Fog-based physiological monitoring.} Erzurumluo\u{g}lu et
al.~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025} deploy emotion recognition on DREAMER with a
Raspberry~Pi~4 fog worker and report the seven transport and resource
metrics we adopt.
Further fog health systems measure latency, energy, bandwidth and
execution time, again without
explanations~\cite{tuli2020healthfog,gia2015}. Conversely, studies that attach explanations to such systems do not
time them (Table~\ref{tab:lit}): LIME and Grad-CAM in a fog emotion
recogniser report throughput only~\cite{elrashidy2025}, a WESAD-trained
1D-CNN with explanations reports no times~\cite{almadhor2025}, and a
LIME-based stress detector on a Jetson edge device reports training
times only~\cite{ghose2025}.

\textbf{The cost of explanation.} ShaTS~\cite{shats2025} states an explicit
real-time criterion---an explainer that needs more than 1\,s per window is
unsuitable for real-time use---and shows KernelSHAP violating it on
workstation hardware. KernelSHAP needs seconds to tens of seconds per
sample on CPUs~\cite{warnecke2020,jethani2022}, whereas amortised
estimators such as FastSHAP~\cite{jethani2022} answer in one forward pass
of a trained explainer, at the price of training it. On a Raspberry~Pi~4,
conditionally triggered SHAP has been reported to add about 195\ms per
prediction in a cold-chain monitoring deployment~\cite{taguta2026}.

\textbf{Selective computation.} Gating computation on softmax confidence is
established for inference through early exits~\cite{teerapittayanon2017}
and confidence cascades~\cite{berestizshevsky2018}, and has been proposed
for explanations as budgeted selective explanation~\cite{paes2024} and as
explanations of rejected samples~\cite{artelt2023}. We claim not the
mechanism but its end-to-end characterisation in continuous physiological
monitoring on constrained hardware, where its benefit depends on
calibration~\cite{guo2017}. Table~\ref{tab:lit} positions this work: none
of the studies times explanations inside a deployed pipeline with the
transport metrics, or gates them on confidence.

\subsection{Model and data}
The classifier is a temporal 1D-CNN with two convolution blocks ([32,\,128]
filters, kernel size 9, BatchNorm--ReLU--MaxPool--Dropout), global average
pooling and two dense layers (\NParams parameters), trained with focal
loss~\cite{lin2017} on 14 chest and wrist channels resampled to 64\,Hz, in
10\,s windows with a 5\,s hop. The architecture reaches 0.92 F1 under
15-fold leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) validation. % OFFLINE
Both were fixed in preliminary offline experiments: window lengths of 1,
30 and 60\,s reached F1 0.816--0.863 against 0.92 for the adopted 10\,s
configuration,
% OFFLINE: docs/refs/tez_pencere_taramasi_cnn.png (1 s: 81.58, 30 s:
% 86.29, 60 s: 83.03; 50% overlap). 0.92 = balanced 10 s run (LOSO).
and a one-factor-at-a-time sweep over depth, filters and kernel size,
scored by LOSO F1 and per-window inference time, preferred the two-block
network to the 105K-parameter baseline (F1 0.931 vs.\ 0.927 at two
thirds of the inference time).
% OFFLINE: docs/refs/offline_sonuclar_30agustos.pdf --
% depth2 [32,128] k9: 49,890 params, ~0.61 ms, F1 0.9307;
% baseline [32,64,128] k9: ~105K, ~0.89 ms, F1 0.9271.
The deployed checkpoint was trained on 13 subjects; the remaining two, S11
and S14, served as the validation set for checkpoint selection (validation
F1 \ValFOne under offline, full-record normalisation). Never used for
training, they are still not independent test subjects; all measurements
below are taken on them. The model is frozen and used unchanged throughout.

\subsection{Real-time pipeline}
The client replays a WESAD record at the sensors' native rates (chest
700\,Hz, wrist 4--64\,Hz) in 500\,ms ticks, each sent as one binary frame of
\PayloadBytes bytes. Resampling to 64\,Hz is performed by the server and is
part of the measured execution time. Offline training normalised every
subject with statistics of the complete record, which uses future data;
the server instead normalises causally (Welford's algorithm on the data
seen so far). Under this constraint the deployed model reaches \RtAcc
accuracy and \RtFOne stress F1 over \RtN labelled windows of S11 and S14 in
an offline replay, and \SelAcc/\SelFOne over \SelNLab labelled windows
of the first \SelMinutes minutes of S11 on the fog node.

\subsection{Transport and measurement}
\label{sec:transport}
\textbf{Single worker with an explicit queue.} A reader task timestamps
every frame on arrival ($t_\mathrm{recv}$) and enqueues it; a single worker
drains the queue ($t_\mathrm{start}$, $t_\mathrm{end}$). We define latency
$=t_\mathrm{recv}-t_\mathrm{send}$, queuing delay
$=t_\mathrm{start}-t_\mathrm{recv}$, execution time
$=t_\mathrm{end}-t_\mathrm{start}$ and response time
$=t_\mathrm{reply}-t_\mathrm{send}$; these definitions follow
\cite{erzurumluoglu2025}. Jitter, the variation of response time
in~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025}, is computed here as the mean absolute
difference of consecutive response times. Bandwidth is
the number of bytes exchanged per second in both directions, and resource
usage is the mean CPU utilisation and peak resident memory (RSS) of the
server process. Nagle's algorithm is disabled (\texttt{TCP\_NODELAY}, the
default of Python's asyncio).

\textbf{Open-loop load.} A client that waits for each reply before the
next tick is a \emph{closed} load generator: when an explanation outlasts
a tick, the emulated sensor pauses and the waiting is omitted from the
measurement---the coordinated-omission problem~\cite{schroeder2006}. In a check with the
200-sample SHAP explainer, a closed-loop client delayed its sends by
\LoopClosedLagPNF\ms at the 95th percentile. Our client is
open-loop---a sender task emits every tick on schedule, independently of
a receiver task---and its send lag on the Pi stayed below \PiLagPNFMax\ms
at the 95th percentile in every single-client run.

\textbf{Clocks.} One-way latency requires both clocks: we estimate the
offset of the two monotonic clocks NTP-style from minimum-RTT ping
exchanges~\cite{mills1991}. Because crystal drift turned one-off
estimates into negative latencies in long runs, the offset is
re-estimated every 60\,s and interpolated linearly per packet; the
resulting uncertainty (half the median minimum RTT) was at most
\ClockUncMax\ms.

\subsection{Explainers and confidence gating}
\textbf{Region-based LIME.} Each $14\times640$ window is divided into 20
time segments per channel (280 regions). A masked region is replaced by the
channel mean; perturbed samples are weighted with an exponential kernel and
fitted by ridge regression. We use 500 samples (\emph{LIME}) and 250
samples (\emph{LIME-cheap}). \textbf{SHAP.} SHAP's GradientExplainer
(expected gradients~\cite{erion2021}) with a fixed 100-window $k$-means
background drawn from training subjects in the causal input space, using
200 samples (\emph{SHAP}) and 50 samples (\emph{SHAP-cheap}). The
operating points come from a one-factor-at-a-time sweep in the same
experiments over LIME's sample count, segments and kernel width and
SHAP's background size, samples and summary method, each setting scored
by faithfulness, monotonicity, sensitivity and latency; the cheap sample
counts kept quality within 4.9\% (LIME) and 1.4\% (SHAP) of the best
setting at 2.3\x and 8.6\x the speed of the largest tried.
% OFFLINE: docs/refs/offline_sonuclar_30agustos.pdf (LIME samples250:
% -4.9%, 2.3x; SHAP nsamples50: -1.4%, 8.6x).
A cheap point runs the same algorithm with fewer samples and costs
proportionally less (\ColocLimeCXaiMed vs.\ \ColocLimeXaiMed\ms for
LIME and \ColocShapCXaiMed vs.\ \ColocShapXaiMed\ms for SHAP on the
laptop; Table~\ref{tab:profile}), with no measurable faithfulness
difference (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-quality}). The
GradientExplainer is not KernelSHAP, whose cost structure
differs~\cite{warnecke2020}.
All random seeds are fixed, and explainers are warmed up when the server
starts, outside the measurement. The model runs with one PyTorch thread;
the numerical libraries used by the explainers (notably BLAS in LIME's
ridge regression) keep their default multithreading on all placements.

\textbf{Gating.} Let $c$ be the maximum softmax probability of a window.
The window is explained if and only if $c<\tau$; $\tau=0$ never explains
and $\tau=1$ always explains.

\textbf{Placements} (Table~\ref{tab:placements}). \emph{Co-located},
client and server run as separate processes on the same laptop over
loopback---the lowest-latency placement, not a wearable. In the \emph{fog} placement the server
runs on a Raspberry~Pi~4 Model~B with active cooling---the same board
model as the fog worker of~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025}, with \FogRAM\,GB of
memory and a \FogGHz\,GHz part against their 8\,GB at 1.8\,GHz. The
\emph{cloud} server runs in Docker on a \CloudCores-vCPU, \CloudRAM\,GB
VPS (Python \CloudPy, SHAP \CloudShapVer) reached over the Internet.

\textbf{Protocol.} All single-client runs replay subject S11 in real time
with causal normalisation; the first 15 windows of every client are
discarded as warm-up. Each configuration is measured in one run; the
CPU governor of each Pi run is stated with the result
(Sec.~\ref{sec:res-gov}). Fixed-policy runs (no explanation, or explanation of every window) last
\AlwMinutes minutes (\FogNoneN windows, the stress-free baseline segment
of the record); selective runs last \SelMinutes minutes (\SelShapCN
windows, spanning baseline and stress); multi-client runs replay S11 and
S14 alternately for 5 minutes. The 10\,s window is the length the model
was trained with, and the 5\,s hop reproduces the 50\% window overlap of
training. We define the \emph{throughput capacity} of a worker as the hop divided
by the mean execution time: the number of clients it keeps up with,
irrespective of individual response times.

\subsection{Explanation dominates the pipeline}
\label{sec:res-cost}
Table~\ref{tab:profile} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cost} profile every
configuration. Without explanation,
the server spends a median of \ColocNoneExecMed\ms per window on the
laptop and \FogNoneExecMed\ms on the Pi, well under 1\% of the 5\,s hop.
On the Pi this divides into \FogNoneResamp\ms of resampling to 64\,Hz,
\FogNonePrep\ms of causal normalisation, \FogNoneTensor\ms of tensor
preparation, \FogNoneInf\ms of inference and \FogNonePost\ms of
post-processing (stage medians; the laptop profile has the same
composition), so every stage fits the real-time budget on both nodes. A single
explanation costs a median of \ColocXaiMin--\ColocXaiMax\ms on the laptop
and \FogXaiMin--\FogXaiMax\ms on the Pi and accounts for
\XaiShareMin--\XaiShareMax\% of execution time in every explained
configuration. Enabling an explainer raises peak server memory by a factor
of \RssGrowMin--\RssGrowMax (e.g.\ from \FogNoneRSS to \FogLimeCRSS\,MB on
the Pi, \FogRssPct\% of its memory). With one client the median queuing
delay is at most \QueMaxAll\ms, and bandwidth is about \KbpsAll\,kbps in
every configuration, because explanations are computed on the server and
not transmitted.

\subsection{Where to explain: compute, not network}
\label{sec:res-place}
The network contributes little on the LAN: the median one-way
latency is \ColocNoneLatMed\ms over loopback and \FogNoneLatMed\ms to the
Pi, and at most \LatMaxAll\ms in any LAN configuration; the Internet path
to the cloud adds \CloudNetMin--\CloudNetMax\ms each way. The
difference between placements comes from compute. Per explanation, the Pi
is \FogOverColocXaiLimeC\x slower than the laptop for LIME-cheap,
\FogOverColocXaiLime\x for LIME, \FogOverColocXaiShapC\x for
SHAP-cheap\ifpishapfull{} and \FogOverColocXaiShap\x for SHAP\fi. Because the slowdown differs between explainers, their ranking
does not transfer across platforms: SHAP-cheap is the fastest explainer on
the laptop and the cloud but slower than LIME-cheap on the Pi. Explaining
\FogOverCloudXaiLimeC--\FogOverCloudXaiShapC\x faster than the Pi, the
cloud answers an explained window sooner despite its Internet path
(\CloudShapCRespMed vs.\ \FogShapCRespMed\ms, SHAP-cheap). All four operating points
meet the real-time criterion of~\cite{shats2025} on the laptop, but on the
Pi only LIME-cheap does (95th percentile \FogLimeCRespPNF\ms): every
SHAP-cheap response exceeds 1\,s (minimum \FogShapCRespMin\ms), and so does
the median LIME response (\FogLimeRespMed\ms)\ifpishapfull{} and the
median SHAP response (\FogShapRespMed\ms)\fi. Under explanation, placement is therefore a decision about the compute
capability of the node.

One caveat on these ratios: LIME-cheap used on
average \FogLimeCCores cores of the Pi while busy (mean CPU \FogLimeCCPU\%
against a busy fraction of \FogLimeCBusy\%), because of multithreaded BLAS;
a single-threaded LIME would be slower.

\subsection{Frequency scaling on the fog node}
\label{sec:res-gov}

The Pi was measured under both of its CPU governors, and the difference
separates the light from the heavy parts of the pipeline.
Under the default on-demand governor, inference took a median of
\FogInfIdle\ms in the run without explanation but \FogInfBusy\ms in runs
where explanations kept the CPU busy; under the performance governor the
difference disappears (\FogInfPerf versus \FogInfBusyPerf\ms), the no-XAI
execution falls from \FogODNoneExecMed to \FogNoneExecMed\ms
(\FogGovGain\x), and the explanation times change by at most
\FogGovXaiLimeC\%. An idle fog node is thus slower than its peak speed for
precisely the cheap requests that dominate when explanations are rare,
whereas the explanations saturate the clock under either governor.
Table~\ref{tab:profile} therefore uses the performance governor; the
selective and multi-client runs (Table~\ref{tab:selective},
Fig.~\ref{fig:load}) ran under on-demand, compared only with
always-explain runs under the same governor.

\subsection{Several users on one fog worker}
\label{sec:res-load}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_load.pdf}
\caption{Pooled response time of one Pi worker serving $N$ synchronised
clients, SHAP-cheap on every window (44 windows per client, on-demand
governor). Bars are measured; black dashes are the queuing model,
$(N{+}1)E/2$ for the mean and $NE$ for the 95th percentile, with $E$ the

\label{fig:load}
\end{figure}

One, two and four clients were run against the Pi with SHAP-cheap on
every window (44 windows per client, pooled; on-demand governor). Started together, the clients' windows complete at the same instants and
their explanations queue back to back: with $N$ clients and mean execution
time $E$ of the run, the response of the $k$-th client served is about
$kE$, the mean response is $(N+1)E/2$, and the 95th percentile approaches
$NE$. The measurements agree with this model within \LoadErrMax\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:load}). At four
clients the worker is \LoadFourUtil\% utilised and keeps up with the load,
as the throughput capacity of \FogODShapCCap users predicts, but the
slowest response approaches the 5\,s hop. Throughput capacity is thus not
a response-time guarantee: already with two clients the 95th-percentile
response is \LoadTwoRespPNF\ms. Synchronised start is the worst case for
this waiting; we did not measure clients with random phases.\footnote{These runs used a server that computed on the event loop
(waiting appeared as latency, not queuing; response time, measured on the
client, is unaffected) and a client whose clock re-synchronisation delayed
single sends by up to \LoadLagMax\,s; both are fixed in the released code.}

\textbf{Several worker processes.} In a separate series on the same Pi
(SHAP-cheap, performance governor, synchronised clients), a server with
$k$ worker processes was compared with $k=1$ measured under the same
conditions. Throughput capacity rose from \MpCapOne to \MpCapTwo and
\MpCapFour clients for $k=1,2,4$ (\MpGainTwo\x and \MpGainFour\x), but each
explanation slowed \MpSlowTwo\x and \MpSlowFour\x. Limiting BLAS to one
thread per process changed explanation time by less than 5\%, which points
to shared memory bandwidth rather than core count. Processes buy users,
not latency: four processes kept up with eight clients (95th-percentile
response \MpFourEightRespPNF\ms), but even an unqueued response took
\MpFourRespMed\ms.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% ======================================================================

\textbf{Where the architectural decision moves.} Without explanation, all
placements answer well within the hop; placement changes response time by
tens of milliseconds at most, as between the edge, fog and cloud
placements of~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025} (22.5, 43.3, 73.3\ms mean). With explanation, two other
parameters dominate: the compute capability of the node, which changes the
cost of an explanation by up to \FogOverColocXaiShap\x, and the triggering
policy, which changes the mean cost by
\SelExecGainMin--\SelExecGainMax\x on our segment. On a Raspberry~Pi~4,
the throughput capacity of one worker
is \FogODShapCCap--\FogODLimeCCap users when every window is explained and
\SelShapCCap--\SelLimeCCap with gating, while individual explained
responses keep their single-user latency only when they do not collide.

\textbf{Reference system.} The client of~\cite{erzurumluoglu2025} also
streams raw signals (16 channels at 128\,Hz, 32 requests/s; 373.9 vs.\
our \KbpsAll\,kbps), but its 3\,s/1\,s windowing and feature-based
classifier make response times not directly comparable.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) All measurements use the two validation subjects
of the deployed checkpoint, and calibration is studied on these two only.
(ii) Each configuration is a single run; within-run statistics rest on
\LoadOneN--\SelShapCN windows, and between-run variance is not reported.
In an earlier offline benchmark (\RepeatRuns runs, laptop, no network or
explanation) run medians varied by \RepeatSpread\% and 95th percentiles by
\RepeatSpreadPNF\%; the effects we draw conclusions from (\FogOverColocXaiLimeC--\FogOverColocXaiShap\x
between placements, \SelExecGainMin--\SelExecGainMax\x from gating) are far
larger. (iii) The placements share the PyTorch release but differ in
processor architecture, operating system, Python and SHAP version; the
placement ratio reflects the whole platform, including multithreaded BLAS
(Sec.~\ref{sec:res-place}); Pi server builds logged no thermal state
(the board was actively cooled).
(iv) The gains of gating were measured on one segment with a
\SelShapCRate\% explanation rate; they scale with the rate.
(v) One server per placement, single-process except in
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-load}; the single-vCPU cloud server leaves no room for
multithreaded BLAS. (vi) The system replays recorded data at native rates; no user study
of explanation usefulness was performed.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ======================================================================

Measured end to end, explanation and not inference is the bottleneck of
real-time explainable stress detection: it takes
\XaiShareMin--\XaiShareMax\% of server execution time, and on a
Raspberry~Pi~4 fog node every 50-sample SHAP explanation takes more than
one second. Confidence-gated explanation reduced the mean cost
\SelExecGainMin--\SelExecGainMax\x on a segment where \SelShapCRate\% of
windows were explained and raised the throughput capacity of a fog worker
by a similar factor, but it directs explanations to errors only where the
model is calibrated. The measurement harness will be made publicly
available.
